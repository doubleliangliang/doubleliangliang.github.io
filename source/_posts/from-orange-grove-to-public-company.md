In the early 1980s, on a hillside outside Meizhou in southern China, a young man walked his own orange grove with a knapsack sprayer on his back, fighting an infestation. By the end of a day the pesticide had soaked through his shirt and burned his back. The trees were still dying.

![From thirty mu of oranges to mist that carries two hundred metres](/images/fenghua-orchard-cover.svg)

That man was Liang Huaxin, home from six years in the navy. He was not setting out to build an industry. He was trying to save two hectares of oranges he had rented and planted himself. The machine he ended up building for that fight is the reason this company exists, and it is still, at bottom, the same machine.

<!-- more -->

## A Soldier Plants Thirty Mu of Trees

Liang Huaxin was born in 1957. At nineteen he left Meizhou for the navy and served in Hainan with a unit garrisoning the Xisha Islands. The heat and the training left him constantly dizzy and headachy, until a local comrade took him home and the comrade's parents scraped powder off a piece of wood, stirred it into hot water and made him drink. The headache went. The wood was agarwood — more on that later.

Home six years later, he worked days at an electronics factory and nights at the municipal food company's slaughterhouse, where he noticed something nobody wanted: the manure. He took it away, rented thirty *mu* of hillside, and planted citrus with it as fertiliser. The trees fruited a year early.

In the third year, the pests came.

## The Pest That Forced the Invention

He did what every grower did: shoulder-mounted sprayer, walk the rows, pump by hand. Slow, wasteful, and most of the chemical landed on the man carrying it. He visited orchards across a dozen counties to see whether someone had solved it. Nobody had. All the market offered was a hand-pumped spray gun.

What he had was a memory from the service: a high-pressure washing rig on the base, used to clean aircraft and airfield equipment, that broke a liquid into a fine fast spray and put it where you aimed. With a mechanical engineer's help, he built his own — a combined pump and sprayer that could draw water for irrigation and spray for crops.

It won a gold medal at the national Spark Programme exposition. What mattered to growers was simpler: work that took five people five days now took two people one day, on less chemical. They came to look, then asked him to build them one.

He had not studied a market and gone looking for a product. He had a problem on his own land, solved it, and the market came to him.

## Two Hundred Square Metres, Borrowed From a Village Co-operative

What he started next was small in a way that is easy to forget: two or three hundred square metres rented from a village co-operative, a dozen workers, and himself as both manager and fitter. Out of that room came stretcher-mounted, self-propelled, hand-pushed, vehicle-mounted, air-assisted, trailed and elevating machines — thirty-odd products for orchards, forestry, landscaping and disinfection.

The predecessor was smaller still: a household-appliance operation called the Fenghua Household Electrical Appliance Works, where Chen Huizhen worked behind the counter. She married Liang Huaxin, came across to run purchasing, and is vice-chairman today. The registered company dates to 1996, as Meizhou Fenghua Spray and Irrigation Machinery Equipment Co., Ltd., with a business scope stating plainly what it made: spray and irrigation machinery for agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry and fishery, plus disinfection, epidemic prevention and deodorisation machinery.

Then the copies appeared. Liang Huaxin had been running the workshop and studying at the same time for years, and his answer was not to cut his prices. He started filing patents. That looks obvious now; it was not, among people whose instinct was to keep their methods quiet. Filing means describing your invention in public in exchange for the right to defend it. He was choosing to make his own products, in the open, for the long run.

## One Technology, Five Problems

Everything this company has ever made sits on one idea: take a liquid, break it into droplets of a controlled size, and put those droplets where they are needed. The pump, the nozzle, the fan, the vehicle, the control system — all of it answers two questions. How far, and how fine.

![One technology, five problems, and the moments that opened each one](/images/spray-technology-five-problems.svg)

**Irrigation and crop protection** came first, in the 1990s, when the government was pushing high-yield, high-quality, high-efficiency agriculture and counties were setting up fruit-tree teams that needed mechanised spraying.

**Disinfection and epidemic prevention** came next, already written into the business scope before anyone needed it. When SARS arrived in 2003, disinfecting large areas suddenly mattered to people who had never thought about droplet size.

**Fire smoke extraction and cooling** followed, using the same throw to pull smoke out of a structure and bring the temperature down.

**Industrial dust** was the fourth. In the north, city and roadside trees ran twenty or thirty metres tall, and nothing on the market could put pesticide that high; the only tool was a fire engine, which used so much water that most of the chemical ended up on the ground, killing insects that were not the target. Liang Huaxin's answer was to put a fan behind the spray. Fall webworm stripped city trees in 2007, and in 2008 Beijing ordered thirty-six of the air-assisted sprayers to fight it across the districts around the capital. That same year he also stood next to an open-pit coal mine in Xinjiang, where the dust was thick enough to hide the sun at midday. Mines, coal yards, steel plants and ports all had the problem the orchard had: particles in the wrong place, and no way to bring them down.

**Urban air** was the fifth, and it changed what the company sells. Holding down PM10 and PM2.5 across a city is not a machine problem. It is truck-mounted units, fixed fog cannons, spray lines on lamp posts along a road, monitoring that shows where the dust comes from, and a maintenance schedule that keeps all of it working. The company stopped selling equipment and started delivering the outcome: survey, design, selection, installation, commissioning, and the service that decides whether it still holds in year four.

Each door opened at a moment: the planting boom of the 1990s, SARS in 2003, the webworm outbreak in 2007, mining dust in 2008. A company can be in the right year and still miss the door. Liang Huaxin had spent the years before it on droplet size.

## The Lonely Fog Cannon

In January 2013, at the Boao Forum for Asia's SME forum in Hainan, Liang Huaxin stood up in a room of entrepreneurs discussing how hard it is to borrow money, and said something that did not fit. He had never needed to borrow. A company does not have to be big, he said; it has to be good at one thing. Twenty years in business, dozens of patents, and every product had paid for itself.

A CCTV host interviewed him on the spot. The channel later made a feature about him and his machine — where he came from, and what the first company into a market runs into: the copying, the infringement, the race to the bottom. They called it *A Lonely Fog Cannon*.

Lonely is the right word, and it is worth being exact about what was lonely.

The first loneliness was having nobody to sell to. For years there was no market for a machine that threw mist two hundred metres. He loaded fog cannons onto trucks and drove to mining districts himself, and came home with nothing sold; a single unit cost more than half a million yuan, and the men he was trying to convince had never bought such a thing and did not yet believe they needed it. Being first into a market that does not exist means paying to create it — the travel, the demonstrations, the years with no orders — and then watching other people walk in once it is worth walking into.

The second is the one that earns the title, and it is entirely domestic. Once the market opened, the imitators arrived within a season. They copied the shape of the machine and the look of the brochure, and then they copied the numbers: throw distances and coverage figures written into specification sheets by people who had never measured one, to win a tender. Some of it was outright infringement. Much of it was harder to argue with — a market where everybody claims two hundred metres and nobody has to prove it, where sellers with no factory and no qualification list machines online at prices he could not match and would not match.

Imitation is cheap, because it only has to reproduce something already proven to work — and cheaper still when nobody checks the claim. Innovation is expensive, and it does not stop. While the imitators were working out how to build last year's machine and print a bigger number on it, he was working out what next year's should do — and there is nobody to talk to while you are doing that. That is the loneliness. Not going unsold. Being the only one in the industry still asking what the machine can actually do, in a market where everyone else is asking how to win the order.

He answered it the only way that holds. He filed patents — dozens of them, invention patents among them — starting the year the copies appeared. He put his equipment in front of an industry federation and a ministry, let them measure it and publish the numbers, so that a claim of two hundred metres meant something anyone could check. He helped write the national standard that defines what good dust suppression is, including for machines he does not build.

A lonely fog cannon is still a fog cannon. It is still working.

## From a Family Workshop to a Public Company

In 2014 the company was restructured into a joint-stock company and its shares began public trading. That was less a financing decision than a statement about what kind of company it meant to be.

![From a rented shed to a public company: four stages](/images/fenghua-family-firm-to-public-company.svg)

A public company has a board, external auditors, published accounts, disclosure obligations and rules on related-party transactions that a family workshop can ignore. It has to explain itself. In an industry where most makers are small and family-run, with no unified product standard and sellers online who have no factory at all, choosing that discipline is a real difference. It is also the inheritance in institutional form: the founder decided the standard in his head should be written down and audited instead.

Liang Huaxin remains chairman and controlling shareholder. Chen Huizhen is vice-chairman. In November 2022 the registered legal representative changed from father to son.

## What the Second Generation Inherited

Liang Liang did not arrive at the top in one step. He graduated from Guangdong University of Foreign Studies and spent two years at a foreign-invested company before he joined his father's. Then he went through the factory floor by floor: a stint in the workshop, then salesman, then the man signing up dealers and agents, then assistant to the general manager — the title he was still carrying in press coverage when the shares began trading in 2014. By 2019 he was the general manager himself, receiving visitors — a provincial science and technology delegation, a university design faculty discussing joint development — and reporting on the company's work. In 2022 he became legal representative. Every step of it happened in public, in a company whose accounts anyone can read.

![What was inherited was values, not titles](/images/two-generations-values.svg)

Asked at that 2019 meeting to describe the company, he described one whose history is a history of independent innovation and research: the years measured not by what had been sold, but by what had been worked out. That is his father's way of talking, not a marketing department's. It is the clearest public evidence of what moved between them, and it was not a title. It was three convictions.

**The technology comes first and the market comes second.** Do not start by asking what is selling.

**The point is the improvement, not the machine.** Two hundred metres means nothing if the dust still reaches the houses.

**Procedures are the product.** Whether a machine still works in its fourth year depends on wear parts replaced on schedule, water quality inside the agreed range, and the anti-freeze routine carried out before the first cold week. None of that appears in a quotation, and all of it is inherited rather than taught.

## What Thirty Years on One Subject Adds Up To

Some of this is worth stating plainly, because it was checked by somebody other than the company.

![Qualifications, standard-setting and intellectual property](/images/fenghua-recognition-and-ip.svg)

- **Standard-setting.** First-listed drafting unit for JB/T 13562–2018, *Air-assisted spraying devices for dust suppression — technical specification*, in force from May 2019. Writing the standard meant defining, in language applying to machines it does not make, what good dust suppression is.
- **Independent assessment.** The China Machinery Industry Federation concluded the dust-suppression equipment had reached internationally advanced level and filled a domestic gap. MIIT published the same verdict: throw beyond 200 m, coverage above 50,000 m², dust concentration down 15–40 per cent, effective suppression above 85 per cent, water use down more than 70 per cent.
- **Qualifications.** National high-tech enterprise; specialised and innovative environmental-equipment enterprise; Guangdong famous trademark; Guangdong contract-honouring, credit-keeping enterprise; ISO 9001; CE marking for export; products tested by the national environmental protection quality supervision and inspection centre.
- **State listings.** Listed by MIIT as meeting the *Industry Normative Conditions for Environmental Protection Equipment Manufacturing (Air Treatment)*, and included in the *Catalogue of Major Environmental Protection Technology Equipment Encouraged by the State*.
- **Intellectual property.** Dozens of granted patents, including invention patents — filings that began the year the copies appeared.
- **Reach.** Exported to eighteen countries and regions, working at ports, mines, steel plants, power stations and city roads. In December 2020 the design and R&D department was named a Guangdong Province Advanced Collective.
- **The founder.** Named a national dual-purpose military-civilian talent; Guangdong science and technology second prize; Guangdong entrepreneur of the year.

None of it is why the company was started. It is what accumulated while it stayed on the same subject.

## The Other Obsession

One more thing about Liang Huaxin does not fit the picture of a machinery manufacturer, and it explains him better than the factory does. He never stopped being the young sailor whose headache was cured by a scrape of agarwood in a comrade's kitchen. For thirty years, alongside the company, he collected it — buying in Vietnam, Cambodia and Indonesia, and eventually building the Shengpin Agarwood Museum in Meizhou to hold it, more than two thousand pieces. He chairs the agarwood branch of the China National Forest Industry Association, and what he says he wants is to keep the carving skill alive and the trees planted.

Thirty years on droplet size, thirty years on a resin that forms inside wounded trees over decades. Both are the same habit: pick something slow, and stay.

## A Company Is Also a Citizen

Liang Liang spends part of his time outside the company too: president of the Meizhou Young Entrepreneurs Association, vice-chairman of the Meizhou Federation of Industry and Commerce, member of the eighth Meizhou Municipal Committee of the CPPCC, president of the Meizhou alumni association of Guangdong University of Foreign Studies, member of the China Young Entrepreneurs Association, member of the Guangdong Provincial Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese, and standing committee member of the Meizhou federation.

As a list, that is a résumé. As an obligation, it is closer to a service record: places where an owner is expected to turn up, listen to what the city needs, and answer for what his industry is doing about it. Dust is slow and diffuse. Sitting in the rooms where people complain about it is how a company finds out what it has been missing.

## What Has Not Changed

The company's stated vision is two phrases long: green mines and quarries, beautiful cities. Translated back to the machine in the orange grove, it is the same sentence — put the water where it does some good.

The man who burned his back trying to save thirty *mu* of oranges would not have called himself an environmentalist. He was a grower with a problem and a memory of a machine. What his son inherited was a company already rebuilt once from a village co-operative's spare room into a public company, and something harder to audit: an insistence that the machine has to work, on site, in year four, whether or not anybody is watching.

Thirty years is long enough to tell whether a company meant it.
